# Power Unit Maintenance Log

The trailer is only half the combination. The **power unit** (truck) needs its own history: oil, brakes, cooling, U-joints, tires, hitch hardware, and tow-package components.

## What this suite provides

On **Power Units**:

1. Register the truck (with OEM rating credits).  
2. **Log truck maintenance** tied to that power unit (open or completed work orders).  
3. Keep miles, vendor, labor/parts cost, and notes offline.

This is **not** a substitute for the manufacturer’s maintenance schedule.

## What you must take from the OEM

| Topic | Publisher |
|-------|-----------|
| Oil grade/interval | Vehicle manufacturer owner manual |
| Brake fluid/service | Vehicle manufacturer |
| Transmission/tow-haul service | Vehicle manufacturer / severe-duty schedule if applicable |
| Torque specs | OEM service information |

## Suggested log entries (examples of titles — not schedules)

- Engine oil and filter  
- Differential / transfer case service  
- Brake inspection  
- Coolant system  
- Tire rotation / replacement  
- Hitch/receiver hardware inspection  
- After heavy tow: check hub temps, transmission temp history if equipped  

Use your OEM severe-duty schedule when you tow often or haul livestock/heat.

## Stop conditions

Not a mechanical procedure. If the truck is unsafe (brakes, steering, overheating), do not tow.

## Pro help triggers

Check-engine under load, transmission slip when towing, brake fade, overheating — qualified shop.
