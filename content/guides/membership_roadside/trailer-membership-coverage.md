# Membership Roadside — What Covers a Trailer?

Auto-club and RV/equestrian **memberships** are not repair shops. They are **dispatch + payment pathways** for roadside help. Whether **your trailer** is covered depends on:

1. **Which organization** (AAA club vs Good Sam vs Coach-Net vs USRider, etc.)  
2. **Which plan tier** (basic car plan often **excludes** heavy trailers/RVs unless you add RV/equestrian coverage)  
3. **What is disabled** (truck only, trailer only, or both)  
4. **What is on the trailer** (especially **live animals**)  
5. **Commercial vs personal use**  

**Always verify with your card, handbook, and a phone call.** STWL does not sell memberships and does not guarantee claim payment.

## Critical trailer realities (all programs)

| Issue | Why it matters |
|-------|----------------|
| **Basic car membership ≠ trailer** | Many base auto plans cover the car/truck only. Trailer/RV often needs an **add-on** or a **trailer-specific** plan. |
| **Truck + trailer is two units** | A dual breakdown may mean **two** service events or only the disabled unit is covered — ask. |
| **Loaded livestock** | Some auto clubs require **animals unloaded** before a tech will work the trailer (welfare + liability). Equestrian plans market **loaded horse trailer** help as a differentiator. |
| **Dollar caps vs mile caps** | Some plans advertise **miles** of towing; others use **dollar limits** per call/year. Heavy RV/trailer tows can exceed dollar caps quickly. |
| **Gooseneck / 5th-wheel / HDT** | Not every local tower can haul a loaded gooseneck. Even with membership, **equipment match** matters. |
| **Not a shop** | Memberships generally **do not pay for parts, major repairs, or new tires** — only dispatch of covered roadside services. |

## AAA (American Automobile Association)

**What it is:** Network of **regional clubs** under the AAA brand. Benefits **are not identical** in every state.

**Trailer / RV angle (public club materials — verify yours):**

- Standard membership is oriented to **passenger vehicles**.  
- **RV / trailer coverage** is often an **upgrade or add-on** (names like Plus RV / Premier RV appear in club marketing).  
- Some club FAQs state that **personal-use trailers** (including **boat, horse, utility**, etc.) may be covered under RV-type benefits when properly enrolled — **licensed/registered, personal use**, loads secured.  
- Some club materials explicitly say **no livestock on board** at service (animals unloaded before technician arrival). That is a major difference from equestrian-specific plans.  
- Public club pages have described **tow distance** benefits (e.g. up to **100** or **200** miles by tier in some materials) **and/or dollar limits** on RV emergency roadside (e.g. per-call / per-year caps in some club disclosures). **Both have been reported** in public club docs and member discussions — **read your current handbook**.  
- **Commercial trailers** and tractor-trailer combinations are generally **out**.

**Action:** Call **your** AAA club with: trailer type (horse/stock/cargo/5th), empty vs loaded animals, and ask for **written plan summary**.

**Contact discovery:** [aaa.com](https://www.aaa.com/) · local club phone on your card.

**Credit:** American Automobile Association / local AAA clubs — official membership and RV FAQ pages.

## Good Sam Roadside Assistance

**What it is:** Membership roadside product marketed heavily to **RVers** (Good Sam / Camping World ecosystem). Separate **RV** and **Auto** plan tracks appear on public Good Sam roadside pages.

**Trailer angle (public marketing):**

- Plans are sold as **24/7** roadside: towing, lockouts, flat-tire service, jump starts, fuel delivery (marketing categories on roadside.goodsam.com).  
- **RV plans** are the track aimed at motorhomes and towables; **auto plans** are for cars/light vehicles — do not assume a cheap auto tier covers a 5th-wheel.  
- Good Sam marketing emphasizes **RV** use cases; **always confirm** whether your exact trailer (travel trailer, toy hauler, cargo, horse) is a covered vehicle type on **your** enrollment.  
- Like others: **parts and major repairs** are generally **not** “free” under roadside.

**Contact discovery:** [roadside.goodsam.com](https://roadside.goodsam.com/) · [goodsam.com](https://www.goodsam.com/)

**Credit:** Good Sam Roadside Assistance — official public website/marketing.

## Coach-Net

**What it is:** **RV-focused** roadside and technical assistance membership (public coach-net.com).

**Trailer angle (public marketing):**

- Marketed for **RV** breakdowns with **24/7** roadside and **RV technical** phone help.  
- Public comparisons often note **towable** vs **motorized** plan options (confirm current plan names/pricing on Coach-Net).  
- Strong differentiator in public discussion: **RV tech support** and helping locate RV-capable service — not just a car-club tow.  
- Still: **parts/tires** usually **member-paid**.

**Contact discovery:** [coach-net.com](https://coach-net.com/)

**Credit:** Coach-Net — official website.

## USRider (Equestrian Motor Plan) — horse / equine clubs path

**What it is:** Roadside membership **built for equestrians** — publicly markets coverage of **tow vehicles and horse trailers**, including messaging about **trailers carrying horses**.

**Trailer / equine angle (public USRider membership pages):**

- Classic-tier public marketing (examples as published on usrider.org — **confirm live numbers**): nationwide coverage; towing for **vehicle and/or horse trailer** (e.g. up to **100 miles** and **$400 max per vehicle per incident** language on public membership pages); roadside service package (tire, battery, fuel/oil/water delivery — excludes cost of parts/fluids); lock-out and winch-out caps listed on plan pages.  
- When trailer is not disabled, public copy describes locating a provider to move a **loaded trailer** to a **safe location**.  
- Equine-oriented extras in public marketing: emergency **vet / stabling / farrier referral** style benefits (referral ≠ free vet bill).  
- **USEF** has historically publicized **member discount** pathways with USRider (2009 USEF press material; **confirm current** offers with USEF/USRider). AQHA and other breed associations have also pointed members to USRider on public pages.  
- **Not a replacement** for horse insurance, hauler commercial coverage, or state livestock transport rules.

**Contact discovery:** [usrider.org](https://usrider.org/) · service phone published on site (e.g. 800-844-1409 on contact pages — verify).

**Credit:** USRider Equestrian Motor Plan — official website; USEF historical partnership press for club linkage.

## Equine clubs & associations (not roadside by themselves)

Breed and sport organizations (examples: **USEF**, **AQHA**, state horse councils, breed associations) typically provide:

- Competition/rules education  
- Sometimes **member discounts** on third-party roadside (e.g. USRider)  
- Emergency response / biosecurity education  

They usually **do not** dispatch a tow truck themselves. Join clubs for sport/community; buy **explicit equestrian roadside** if you haul horses.

## Practical decision guide (trailer haulers)

| You mainly haul… | Ask about first |
|------------------|-----------------|
| **Travel trailer / 5th-wheel living unit** | Good Sam RV track, Coach-Net, AAA **RV** add-on |
| **Cargo / utility / empty stock** | AAA club trailer rules; Good Sam RV vs auto; dollar caps |
| **Horse trailer (especially loaded)** | **USRider** (or equivalent equestrian plan); confirm AAA livestock unload rules |
| **Commercial hauling** | Membership often **excludes** commercial use — need commercial towing contracts |

## What is usually **not** covered (all brands — confirm)

- Cost of **new tires**, major **parts**, shop **labor** for repairs  
- **Storage** fees after drop-off  
- **Unsecured** loads / illegal configurations  
- **Commercial** use when plan is personal only  
- Guarantees that a local tower can handle **gooseneck + livestock**

## Stop conditions

- Assuming base car membership covers a loaded gooseneck horse trailer without written confirmation  
- Leaving livestock on a trailer when the provider requires unload  
- Relying on membership instead of fixing brakes, tires, or overload  

## Pro help triggers

- Commercial livestock transport  
- Multi-day breakdown with animals aboard  
- Disputes over coverage — contact the membership provider and document the claim number
