# Manufactured Housing / Mobile-Home Axles — How to ID Them

**Manufactured housing (MH) / mobile-home transport axles** are built primarily to **move a home once** (or a few factory moves), not as general-purpose highway service axles. They show up constantly on **homemade trailers** and **emergency repairs** because they are cheap and available after a home is set.

This guide helps you **recognize** them and record the right **axle type** in the suite. It does **not** invent a GAWR.

## Why this matters

- Many MH-style assemblies are **harder to service** (welded components, special rims).  
- Tires may be marked for **MH / single-trip style use** — read the sidewall.  
- Homemade trailers often **shorten** long MH beams (weld in the middle).  
- **Never invent capacity** — if there is no tag, treat capacity as unknown and plan accordingly.

## Family of trailer axle types (quick map)

| Style | What you usually see | Typical use |
|-------|----------------------|-------------|
| **Leaf-spring service axle** | Beam + leaf packs; hubs often bolt-on; common lug-centric wheels | Cargo, stock, RV service trailers |
| **Torsion (rubber cord)** | No leaves; rubber elements in the tube; independent arm feel | Many modern light trailers (OEM dependent) |
| **Manufactured housing / mobile-home transport** | Clamp-on / centerless rims common; often welded brake hardware; may be very wide or shortened | Home transport; frequently reused on DIY decks |
| **Drop vs straight spindle** | Drop lowers deck; straight keeps higher stance | Builder choice on leaf-type beams |
| **Commercial heavy beam** | Large brakes, hub-piloted duals, air hardware | Medium/heavy equipment trailers |

**Most common on ordinary light trailers (new OEM cargo/RV):** leaf-spring or torsion **service** axles from large light-trailer OEMs — **Dexter** and **Lippert (LCI)** are among the names most often seen on North American light/RV trailer tags (credit: manufacturer presence in the market; always read *your* tag).

**Most common cheap DIY / emergency swap material:** **MH / manufactured-housing transport axles** scavenged after set-up (field consensus from owners/techs who inspect homemade equipment).

## How to identify MH / mobile-home transport axles

No single clue is 100% by itself. Stack clues:

### 1. Wheels and rims (strong clue)

- **Centerless / clamp-on rims** that clamp to a large drum/hub instead of a normal multi-lug passenger-style wheel.  
- Common **14.5″** MH wheel size on many transport setups.  
- **Caution:** Some legitimate commercial lowboy/utility designs also use clamp-style hubs — do not stop at rims alone.

### 2. Tires (strong clue when original)

- Sidewall language such as **MH**, **manufactured home**, or **single-use / limited use** style markings (wording varies by tire maker).  
- DIY builders often leave these tires on when recycling the axle.

### 3. Brake backing plate (strong clue)

- On many MH axles, the **brake backing plate is welded** to the axle tube rather than bolted to a service flange.  
- Welded-on hardware often means **not designed for routine service replacement** like a Dexter-style service axle.

### 4. Beam length and mid-tube weld (very strong on DIY trailers)

- Factory MH beams are often **wider** than a highway trailer wants.  
- Homemade builders frequently **cut and splice** the tube to narrow the track. A **weld/splice in the middle of the beam** is a classic sign of a shortened MH axle.

### 5. Trailer paperwork

- Homemade trailer with no reputable trailer OEM plate + clamp rims → high chance of MH hardware.  
- Branded trailer with VIN and OEM axle tags (Dexter/Lippert/etc.) → usually **service** axles; still verify tags.

### 6. Axle ID tag (gold standard when present)

- Look on the **axle tube** for a riveted/stamped tag (capacity, serial, description).  
- **Lippert** and other OEMs publish how to read their tags for *their* product lines — use the tag, not internet guesses.  
- Many pure MH transport pieces have **poor or missing rating tags**; absence of a tag is a warning, not a free pass.

## Who is “most common”?

| Context | What shows up most often | How we know (credit) |
|---------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| **New light cargo / RV trailers** | Dexter, Lippert, and similar **service** leaf or torsion lines | OEM product distribution and ID-tag practice (Dexter, Lippert public materials) |
| **Homemade / yard-built decks & emergency swaps** | **Manufactured-housing transport axles** (often untagged or MH-tired) | Field identification consensus (public owner/tech forums such as TractorByNet MH axle threads) |
| **Commercial equipment trailers** | Larger commercial brands / hub-piloted duals | Commercial axle OEM literature (Meritor, SAF-Holland, Hendrickson, etc.) |

STWL does **not** publish a market-share percentage. “Most common” above is **context-dependent** and credited as field/OEM presence, not a census.

## Single wheel vs dual wheel

- **Single** = one tire per side on that axle.  
- **Dual (dually)** = two tires per side.  
- Capacity is **not** “dual = automatic upgrade” — use the **GAWR stamped for that axle assembly**.  
- MH transport setups are often **single** with clamp rims; duals appear more on heavier service/commercial beams.

## Recording in this suite

On **Trailers**, for each axle (1–5 / quint):

1. Set **Axle type / style** → *Manufactured housing / mobile-home transport axle* when clues match.  
2. Set **Wheel end** single or dual.  
3. Enter **GAWR from the plate** if present; leave blank if unknown (traffic light will flag missing axle ratings).  
4. Credit the **axle tag publisher** (or “untagged MH salvage”).  
5. Note splices, welded backing plates, and MH tire wording in notes.

## Safety / legal judgment (plain English)

- MH hardware may be **legal or not** for your use depending on **state inspection**, width, tires, brakes, and how it was modified — STWL does not certify trailers.  
- If you cannot establish a credible **GAWR** and safe brakes/tires, do not treat the combination as a green tow.  
- Prefer **service-duty** axles with replaceable hubs/bearings for trailers you use often on public roads.

## Stop conditions

- No tag and no credible capacity  
- MH “single-use” tires used as daily highway tires without replacement  
- Cracked mid-tube splice welds  
- Brakes that cannot be serviced or do not hold  

## Pro help triggers

- Structural weld repair on axle tubes  
- Bearing/spindle damage on welded-hub designs  
- Any doubt before hauling heavy equipment or livestock
