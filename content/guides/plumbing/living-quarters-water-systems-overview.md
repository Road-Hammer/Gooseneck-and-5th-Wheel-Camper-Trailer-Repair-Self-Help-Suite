# Living-Quarters Water Systems — Fresh, Gray, Black

Campers and trailers with **living quarters** (kitchen, bath, sometimes laundry) almost always use a **three-system** layout:

| System | What it is | Where it goes |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| **Fresh** | Clean water for drinking/cooking/washing | Onboard tank and/or **city-water** hose |
| **Gray** | Sink and shower drain water | Gray holding tank → dump valve |
| **Black** | Toilet waste | Black holding tank → dump valve |

Cargo-only or stock trailers usually have **none** of this. Horse trailers with **living quarters (LQ)** do.

## Fresh water — two ways in

1. **City water inlet** — pressurized hose from campground spigot (use a **pressure regulator** if your manual recommends one; many do to protect fittings).  
2. **Onboard fresh tank + 12 V pump** — pump builds pressure when a faucet opens (and when the pump switch is on).

**Never** cross-connect drinking water to a hose that has been used for sewer without proper cleaning. Use dedicated **potable** hoses for fresh.

## Gray and black — two ways out

- Separate **pull valves** at the dump fittings (usually under the wet bay).  
- **Black first, then gray** is common practice so gray water rinses the hose — confirm your owner routine.  
- Dump only at **approved dump stations** or dump facilities. Local health rules apply.

## What “living quarters” adds

| Equipment | Notes |
|-----------|--------|
| Toilet | Cassette, gravity flush, or macerating — **follow that toilet’s manual** |
| Water heater | Electric, LP, or both — tank or tankless |
| Pump | 12 V demand pump + often an accumulator |
| Filter | In-line or canister on fresh side (optional aftermarket) |
| Low-point drains | For winterizing and full drain-down |

## Safety

- **Sewage is a biohazard** — gloves, eye protection, wash hands.  
- **LP water heaters and appliances** — gas leaks are life-safety; propane work has hard pro boundaries (see propane category when published).  
- **Leaks into floors** cause rot and delamination — treat slow drips as urgent.

## Service log fields

## Stop conditions

- Active sewage leak into living space  
- Fresh tank contaminated with antifreeze or sewage (do not drink)  
- Smell of LP near water heater while investigating plumbing  

## Pro help triggers

- Tank or toilet replacement  
- Structural floor damage from leaks  
- Persistent sewer odor after proper dump/vent check
