# Winterizing Living-Quarters Plumbing (Entry-Level)

Freezing water **splits pumps, heaters, and PEX**. Winterizing is mandatory for coaches stored or used below freezing unless the unit is continuously heated and verified.

## Two common methods (OEM chooses)

| Method | Idea | Notes |
|--------|------|--------|
| **RV antifreeze** | Pump non-toxic **RV** antifreeze through lines until pink (or product color) shows at faucets | Most common DIY path |
| **Compressed air blow-out** | Blow lines dry, then often a small antifreeze charge in traps | Requires correct regulator and method — easy to damage if over-pressured |

**Your owner manual wins.** Some heaters need **bypass**; some tankless units have special steps.

## Critical safety rules

1. **Never** put **automotive engine coolant (ethylene glycol)** in the fresh-water system.  
2. Use antifreeze **labeled for RV/potable plumbing** when the method requires liquid in the lines.  
3. Turn **water heater off** and cool before draining.  
4. Dump **black and gray** before storage when practical.  
5. Battery/converter: pumps need 12 V if using the coach pump to pull antifreeze.

## Typical antifreeze winterize outline (generic)

*Follow your OEM diagram for valve handles.*

1. Drain fresh tank; open low-point drains; drain water heater if OEM says so.  
2. Close low points; set **water heater bypass** to bypass if equipped.  
3. Insert pump inlet into antifreeze jug (or use winterize valve to siphon).  
4. Run each faucet (hot and cold) until antifreeze appears; flush toilet; run outside shower if fitted.  
5. Bypass or protect ice makers/washers per appliance manuals.  
6. Pour a little antifreeze in sink/shower **P-traps** if required by your climate/method.

## De-winterize (spring)

1. Drain remaining antifreeze from tank/lines per manual.  
2. Sanitize fresh system if your OEM or water-quality practice requires it (chlorine method per coach manual — do not invent concentrations here).  
3. Flush until clear; re-open heater path; check for leaks under pressure.

## What this does **not** protect

- **Slide seals, roofs, and batteries** need their own cold-weather care.  
- **Tires and suspension** still age in storage.  
- **Animals / cargo** — different rules.

## Service log

- Date winterized / method (antifreeze brand or air)  
- Water heater bypass used Y/N  
- De-winterize / sanitize date

## Stop conditions

- You cannot find water heater drain/bypass and guess would force fittings  
- Unit will be lived in unheated in deep freeze without a proven heat strategy  

## Pro help triggers

- First winterize on an unfamiliar floorplan  
- Tankless water heater or complex whole-coach filtration  
- Burst lines already present
