# Fresh Water — Pump, City Inlet, and Common Leaks

## Scope

Covers:

- Why the pump runs or will not  
- City water vs tank mode  
- Finding drips on the **fresh** side  

Does **not** cover black-tank seals, full water-heater tank swap, or structural floor rebuilds.

## City water vs pump mode

| Mode | What happens |
|------|----------------|
| **City water** | Hose pressurizes the coach; pump usually **off** |
| **Tank + pump** | Pump switch **on**; pump cycles when a faucet opens |

Common mistakes:

- Pump left **on** while on city water (can fight pressure or cycle oddly depending on check valves).  
- Pump **on** with empty tank (runs dry — can damage many pumps).  
- No **pressure regulator** on high campground pressure (burst fittings).

## Pump will not run — quick checks

1. Pump switch on panel **on**.  
2. Battery / converter power good (12 V at pump when switch on and a faucet open).  
3. Fuse for pump circuit.  
4. Strainer screen clogged (many pumps have an inlet strainer).  
5. Tank empty or tank valve closed.  
6. Pump air-locked after dry run or filter change — prime per pump manual.

## Pump runs but no water / weak flow

1. Kinked hose or closed shutoff under sink.  
2. Clogged aerator screens at faucet.  
3. Filter cartridge plugged.  
4. Frozen line (cold weather).  
5. Failed check valve or collapsed tank outlet hose.

## City water — no flow or leaks at inlet

1. Spigot on; hose not kinked.  
2. Pressure regulator orientation (arrow toward coach if marked).  
3. City-water check valve stuck or cracked — wet bay drip.  
4. **Never** force a cross-threaded garden-hose fitting.

## Finding leaks (entry-level)

1. Dry the wet bay and under sinks.  
2. Pressurize (city water **or** pump with tank).  
3. Watch fittings, pump head, water heater cold inlet, filter housings, PEX crimps.  
4. Check **low-point drains** not seating.  
5. Ceiling stains → upper-level line or roof leak (roof is not always “plumbing”).

## DIY fixes that stay entry-level

- Tighten accessible fittings **hand-tight plus slight snug** — plastic cracks if over-torqued.  
- Replace cheap garden-hose washers and cracked pressure regulators.  
- Clean pump strainer.  
- Replace a clearly split section of accessible tubing **if** you have the correct fittings and tools.

## Pro help triggers

- Hidden leak under floor or behind walls  
- Water heater tank leak  
- Repeated pump failures after dry running  
- Contaminated fresh tank (antifreeze, sewage, heavy mold)

## Service log

Date, city vs tank mode problem, parts (pump, regulator, fittings), whether leak was at wet bay / sink / heater.
